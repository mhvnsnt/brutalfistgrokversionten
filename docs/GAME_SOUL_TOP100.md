## The 10 Design Laws

### 1. PLAYERS FIRST, NOT PAYERS
Tim Sweeney's exact words — "Fortnite is for players, first and foremost" — echo across all 100 studios. Take-Two's Strauss Zelnick: "focused on players, not payers" — get captivation right and "the monetization will take care of itself." Supercell's Paananen: "if monetisation is not your number one priority, that actually leads to better monetisation" and "you can't design fun on a spreadsheet." Dream Games (Royal Match): "We have never focused on monetisation. We strongly believe that if we have a high-quality experience people will pay."
**Law:** Optimize for the player's joy. Revenue is a lagging indicator of love.

### 2. MONETIZE LOVE, NOT POWER
Genshin's Liu Wei coined it: "paying for love" — sell affection for characters, never rank-climbing power. Fortnite: "no pay-to-win... no scenario in which spending gives you a benefit." Guild Wars 2: never OK to sell advantage. Team Fortress 2 went cosmetics-only and made **12x revenue**. Brawl Stars deleted all loot boxes to be "more fair and predictable." Path of Exile's ethical F2P: "never tie game design to monetization." Helldivers 2's Pilestedt: "earn the right to monetize."
**Law:** Style, not power. Always. (This is already our binding law — the giants validate it.)

### 3. GENEROSITY IS A GROWTH STRATEGY
Vampire Survivors: new SYSTEMS always free; paid DLC = content only; base game priced like a coffee. GTA Online: a decade of free updates because paid DLC "fractured the audience." Balatro: one fair price, no microtransactions, the whole designed experience. Minecraft: stewardship of 15-year-old player worlds — "we want to make sure that they can trust us." Rocket League: free updates, platform-not-franchise.
**Law:** Give more than expected. Generous games get evangelized for free.

### 4. COMMUNITY AS CO-DESIGNER, NOT AUDIENCE
Rockstar: "a beautiful partnership with the community." Supercell: "building a game together with our community, rather than for them" — Reddit jokes ship straight into Brawl Stars. Madden: ≥50% of yearly changes come from player requests ("all feedback is a gift"). Minecraft: community is "our strongest inspiration." RAID: "community is a vehicle for new ideas." EVE: players cleaning the sandbox themselves is "a beautiful thing."
**Law:** Build WITH players. Ship their ideas visibly; credit them loudly.

### 5. TRUST THE PLAYER
Miyazaki (Elden Ring): freedom of progression, exploration built on trust. Josef Fares: never repeat a mechanic — "$1,000 if someone genuinely thinks they get tired of It Takes Two." Larian: "player first, always," no-rails agency. Palworld: "let players build their own story." Sakurai: fun over fairness. The Witness-era lesson repeats everywhere: players are smarter than tutorials assume.
**Law:** Never patronize. Give systems, not rails; let players surprise you.

### 6. MAKE IT FOR YOURSELF FIRST
Galante: "Vampire Survivors is the first game I made just for fun. That might be the key ingredient." miHoYo's Cai: "make anime games that were exciting for us." Black Myth's Feng Ji: satisfy devs first; "fun as goal and bottom line." SYBO (Subway Surfers): "Fun always comes first."
**Law:** If the team isn't having fun, players won't either. Build the game you'd camp outside a store for.

### 7. ACCESSIBILITY IS RESPECT
Monopoly GO deliberately removed skill barriers so "your mother might play much more than you." Free Fire: runs on the phones people actually own. Celeste: brutally hard but secretly kind — mechanics "fudged a tiny bit in the player's favor," Assist Mode that never shames. Tekken's Harada: "easy to get into, but a lot to learn and master — that's why Tekken keeps people hooked." Animal Crossing: "be who they want and enjoy the games how they like."
**Law:** Meet players where they are — devices, skill, time. Never shame; always invite.

### 8. FRESHNESS WITHOUT BETRAYAL
Live games stay alive through deliberate disruption — Respawn "tends the garden," Apex "resets the mastery curve," Fortnite's live events. BUT: Mojang's stewardship rule — 15-year-old worlds must keep working; "we want to make sure that they can trust us." Slay the Spire: constraints (drafting every run) create infinite replayability without invalidating anything. Among Us: "update the game people already play."
**Law:** Keep it fresh, never break what players love. Novelty on top of permanence.

### 9. EARNED DIFFICULTY, HONEST REWARDS
Brawl Stars killed loot boxes for deterministic progression. Clash Royale devs seek the "sweet spot between pay to win and pay to lose." Diablo-style honesty: show odds, honor effort. Phasmophobia: "progression is knowledge, not gear" — "we'll never call ourselves a live service." Dead Cells: "don't force variety — seduce via curiosity."
**Law:** Rewards must feel earned and legible. No dark patterns, no treadmills disguised as progress.

### 10. KNOW WHEN TO KILL, KNOW WHEN TO STOP
Supercell shut down Squad Busters (Oct 2026) rather than let it limp — the kill decision IS the philosophy: protect the bar. Galante: "It would be crazy for me to try and do something bigger just because it's what everyone is expecting... one little step ahead, but just a little one." Fares: finish the game instead of stretching it.
**Law:** Protect quality over sunk cost. A clean ending beats a slow rot; a small step beats a bloated leap.

## Monetization Doctrine (what the 100 agree on)
- **Cosmetics only.** Power is never for sale. (Fortnite, TF2, Brawl Stars, GW2, Genshin "paying for love")
- **No loot boxes / no hidden odds.** Deterministic rewards. (Brawl Stars 2022, Sweeney's rejection)
- **Free systems, paid content.** New mechanics belong to everyone. (Vampire Survivors, GTA Online)
- **One fair price is a superpower** for premium-feel games. (Balatro, Minecraft, Stardew Valley)
- **Earn the right first.** Ship the full game, then ask. (Helldivers 2, Lethal Company $10 no DLC)
- **Never fracture the audience** with paid gates. (GTA Online's free-update doctrine)

## What keeps people COMING BACK (the retention core)
Across genres, the same five: **(1)** friends/social ritual (Honor of Kings 5v5, Monopoly GO sticker trading, WoW's community); **(2)** self-expression (Minecraft, Roblox, character skins); **(3)** mastery with no ceiling (Tekken, Celeste, Dead Cells); **(4)** surprise/novelty inside familiarity (Fortnite events, Slay the Spire drafts, Vampire Survivors builds); **(5)** a world that respects their time (Brawl Stars 3-min sessions, Vampire Survivors auto-attacks, ZZZ anti-FOMO).

## What this means for CONCRETE DRAGON specifically
- **Style-not-power is the billion-dollar consensus**, not a handicap. Every top earner that kept its soul sells identity, never advantage.
- **The street is the IP.** Honor of Kings roots heroes in culture people claim; GTA Online sells the living-street fantasy. Our edge: REAL streets, the owner's books and characters, a community with fingerprints on the game.
- **Accessibility = our market.** Free Fire proved the next billion players are on modest phones. Our single-file HTML build already runs anywhere — that IS the strategy.
- **Generosity compounds.** Free updates, free systems, fair prices. The tip jar + PWYW supporter packs fit the doctrine exactly.
- **Community as co-designer.** The owner's playtest-driven process ("you play it, you say keep or cut") is literally Supercell's and Rockstar's stated method. Keep it.
- **Infinite game, honest grind.** The infinite-missions vision needs Slay-the-Spire-style constraints (daily seeds, modifiers) so infinity stays interesting, plus Phasmophobia's rule: mastery, not treadmill.
