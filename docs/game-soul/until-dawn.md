# Until Dawn — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Supermassive Games; published by Sony Interactive Entertainment
- Genre / platforms: Interactive drama / teen-slasher horror; PS4 (2015)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): The Butterfly Effect — thousands of choices producing hundreds of endings, where even trivial-seeming choices ripple hours later. SCEE product manager Tim Hodges: "There are thousands of potential branches to every player's individual story... You won't always know what impact your choices will have until after you've made them — it was important to us that players shouldn't second guess their path through the story of Until Dawn." Death is permanent: "If your actions cause the character you are controlling or another one of the friends to die, the game does not reload. The story continues, the plot irrevocably changed." Director Pete Samuels' positioning (via PlayStation LifeStyle interview) centers replayability on "hundreds of endings... designed to be played multiple times." Community sharing ("No two people on the planet will take the same path," per director Will Byles, via Press-Start) turns each run into a story worth telling.

- Why players love/support it: It's a playable horror movie with friends watching — couch co-op voting on choices became a cultural ritual. The no-reload death rule gives every quick-time event genuine stakes; replaying to save everyone (or kill everyone) is the core loop.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Premium single-purchase; no on-record dev monetization philosophy found.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Consequences you can't undo create stakes no mechanic can fake — consider an "ironman" style no-reload mode where losses are permanent and the story continues. Make even small choices ripple later so every playthrough feels unique enough to talk about.
