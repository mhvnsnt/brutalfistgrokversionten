# Battlefield 1 — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: DICE (Electronic Arts)
- Genre / platforms: Military FPS (WWI); PC, PS4, Xbox One (premium release, 2016)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Personal, human-scale War Stories told as an anthology of focused character vignettes rather than one hopping protagonist — immersion and emotional engagement first; multiplayer sandbox freedom (multiple ways to complete any objective, improvisation, dynamic weather, destruction) keeps each match feeling different.
- Key quotes:
  - "While our characters are at war, the stories in *Battlefield 1* are personal. They're about people rather than history or battles." — DICE, official Battlefield 1 blog post (via GameSpot, 2016)
  - "We wanted the player to see and feel what the characters are going through, rather than just experiencing it from behind their eyes. That has really payed off for us not just in storytelling ability, but in emotional engagement." — DICE, official Battlefield 1 blog post (via GameSpot, 2016)
  - "We felt that to have one character hopping through those different settings wouldn't be as immersive or totally respectful to the setting. So we decided on an anthology format; a set of characters with their own more focused stories. That way we can have immersion and variety — a double win." — DICE, official Battlefield 1 blog post (via GameSpot, 2016)
  - "We were extremely nervous about whether or not people would like the concept. Thankfully the internet exploded and from that point on the game has been surrounded by an enormous positive energy, and that's something that makes me super happy." [unverified] — Lars Gustavsson, Senior Producer, DICE (via FandomWire, search snippet, undated; not from a fetched page)
- Why players love/support it: A rare mainstream shooter that treated war as human tragedy instead of spectacle; the bold WWI setting with horses, biplanes and trench warfare felt fresh against modern-shooter fatigue; still-populated 64-player Conquest years later; the iconic opening (your soldier dies in the mud) is one of gaming's most remembered intros.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): No on-record developer statement on monetization philosophy found for Battlefield 1. ANALYSIS: premium buy-to-play with Premium Pass map expansions.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Anthology storytelling > one hero doing everything — short, focused, respectful character vignettes earn emotional engagement that a single linear campaign can't. In multiplayer, "there are a number of ways to complete the same objective" is the retention engine: design objectives with 3+ valid approaches, never one.
