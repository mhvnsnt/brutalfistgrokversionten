# Dota 2 — Heart and Soul

- Developer / studio: Valve (with IceFrog as lead designer)
- Genre / platforms: Competitive MOBA (5v5) / PC (Steam)

- Why players come back (in the devs' view): The game is designed around unforgettable emergent moments — the "amazing comeback" that would be a "robbery of fun" to lose — and around every player creating value for the other nine in the match ("every single person in the game as creating user generated content"). The dev team treats Dota as a product built to last: "We just assume that it's going to be around forever."

- Key quotes:
  - "One of the things we never want to lose is the amazing comeback... both sides probably would have surrendered in that game at certain points. That would have been a robbery of fun." — Erik Johnson, Dota 2 Project Lead, PC Gamer, 2013
  - "We look at every single person in the game as creating user generated content. A person who just plays the game is generating some value for the other nine people playing." — Erik Johnson, Dota 2 Project Lead, PC Gamer, 2013
  - "We look at Dota as a product whose lifespan is further out than we can think about usefully. We just assume that it's going to be around forever." — Erik Johnson, Dota 2 Project Lead, PC Gamer, 2013

- Why players love/support it: Matches produce once-in-a-lifetime emergent narratives ("the reward for learning to play is learning to see"); the community trusts Valve not to break the game with monetization; the pro scene and Workshop let players and creators share in the value they generate.

- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): The "regret test" — any system that earns money at the cost of customer happiness is "a broken system that we need to fix." "It's our belief — and we have a lot of data to back up this belief — that products that use microtransactions or economic systems as tools for maximising revenue from unhappy users will lose in the marketplace." "We would rather someone not become a customer of ours, rather than them become a customer and be unhappy about that decision." — Kyle Davis, Valve, Steam Dev Days talk, via PCGamesN, 2013. All heroes free; monetization is cosmetic-only (Battle Pass, Workshop revenue share).

- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Build for comeback moments and emergent stories, not just wins — players remember the match they almost lost more than the ones they dominated. Monetize only where nobody can feel regret: no pay-to-win, no artificial barriers sold back.
