# Fire Emblem: Three Houses — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Intelligent Systems + Koei Tecmo Games (Team Ninja/Omega Force team) + Nintendo; co-directors Toshiyuki Kusakihara and Genki Yokota
- Genre / platforms: Tactical RPG + monastery life-sim; Nintendo Switch (2019)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Your choices become history — "The core of Fire Emblem: Three Houses was telling a dramatic story about what happens when people with strong, uncompromising beliefs collide," so the three-route structure was "inevitable." No canonical "correct" route exists: "each route is its own history, and we wanted players to be able to decide that for themselves." Players surprise the devs by finishing all routes ("more people ended up playing all of the routes than we had originally expected") and then debate them — the game is built to be argued over.
- Key quotes: (2-4; each: "quote" — Name, Role, Publication/Event, Year; append [unverified] if from a search snippet not a fetched page)
  - "In Three Houses, each route is its own history, and we wanted players to be able to decide that for themselves. In this game, the leaders of the three houses have their own unwavering beliefs and worldviews... We wanted to model the story and its themes after a dilemma that was grounded in reality." — Toshiyuki Kusakihara, Co-director, Famitsu (via NintendoEverything), 2020
  - "We had thought that everybody would play around with one of the routes and then enjoy asking each other about their respective routes. It was surprising, though; more people ended up playing all of the routes than we had originally expected." — Toshiyuki Kusakihara, Co-director, Famitsu (via NintendoEverything), 2020
  - "Each of the countries has its own values and sense of right and wrong, and it's impossible to decisively say which is good and which is evil." — Yosuke Hayashi, Producer (Koei Tecmo), Game Informer (Three Hopes interview), 2022 [unverified]
- Why players love/support it: The bond system (teaching/beloved students) makes every unit feel personal, and permadeath stakes turn choices into consequence; players who finish all routes get "three-dimensionality to the story" (Hayashi). Maddening mode timed after the main story gave veterans a reason to replay ("why don't I go through it again"). The Cindered Shadows side story DLC answered the hunger for more of the same world without diluting the three-route dilemma.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Standard Nintendo single-purchase + Expansion Pass with a story side chapter; no live-service mechanics — value = "three times as much game" without skimping per route.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Branch the story into genuinely irreconcilable perspectives with no canonical "correct" path — players replay and debate when the choice has real stakes and no golden route. Reward replays with harder modes timed after the main run.
