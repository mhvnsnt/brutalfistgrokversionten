# Guilty Gear Strive — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Arc System Works (directors Daisuke Ishiwatari, Akira Katano)
- Genre / platforms: 2.5D anime fighting game / PS4, PS5, PC (Steam), Xbox One/Series, Switch (2025)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): The directors framed Strive as a deliberate reset — lower the first-impression barrier so newcomers can enjoy it while keeping deep, expressive mechanics for veterans. Katano argues new-player retention comes not from dumbing the game down but from skill-matched competition (the floor/tower rank system) and spectator legibility: "simple" and "easy" are separate concepts. Ishiwatari centers "mischief" — hidden depth and player freedom — as the series' soul, and "wild balance" over mathematical fairness: characters should be *fun*, even at the cost of tier parity.
- Key quotes:
  - "When we're making a game, we want to include a lot of mischief. So when the players find this mischief, the game becomes fun for them. There's value in going deeper into the game because in situations you wouldn't normally realize, we've added as many interesting things there as we can." — Daisuke Ishiwatari, General Director, JAEPO interview (via EventHubs, 2020)
  - "We've been using a word called 'wild balance,' which says that when we're balancing the game we don't talk about what's strong or weak, but instead what's fun or boring and try to focus on that. We sharpen certain areas and in exchange we lower other areas. We basically balance from an equivalent exchange perspective." — Daisuke Ishiwatari, General Director, 4Gamer interview (via EventHubs, 2021)
  - "I believe that the solution to getting new players to enjoy matches is not to make the game itself easier, but rather to create a rank system that makes it easy for them to play with others who are around the same skill level. If players can fight others at the same level, there's no need to try and make new players play the same way as advanced players, nor to make game mechanics that obscure the gap in their skill levels." — Akira Katano, Director, Developer's Backyard, Aug 2020
  - "We forget all traditions from past titles for once and reconsider how Guilty Gear ought to be like; that's how we're developing this game." — Daisuke Ishiwatari, General Director, Famitsu interview (via Siliconera, 2020)
- Why players love/support it: The cinematic "anime come to life" visuals (Ishiwatari's "Hollywood" ambition), rollback netcode from day one (rare for ArcSys), the soundtrack and world-building, and an accessible entry ramp that kept depth. Post-launch seasons of characters (Bridget, etc.) and ArcSys's Developer's Backyard direct communication built trust.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Premium game + season passes (character DLC), no live-service shop; Ishiwatari's launch-interview line acknowledges the business tension — "development costs go up while the user count goes down. We want to have many people recognize the Guilty Gear brand and play the game" — i.e., monetization flows from *audience growth*, not extraction. On cosmetics: "We don't have any plans for it presently. Our current priority is to add more characters to the roster." (toptier.gg dev interview)
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Design for "fun first, balance later" — make every character excitingly distinct ("wild balance") and let spectators understand what's happening, because watchability is marketing. Prioritize the network/online experience (rollback-quality) before squeezing players with purchases.
