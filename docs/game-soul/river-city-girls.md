# River City Girls — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: WayForward Technologies (director Adam Tierney; assistant director/combat designer Bannon Rudis), supervised by Arc System Works
- Genre / platforms: Beat-'em-up with RPG progression / Switch, PS4/PS5, Xbox One/Series, PC; sequel (RCG2) 2022
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): WayForward fused two loyalties — fighting-game depth and Kunio-kun street-brawling heart. Combat designer Bannon Rudis: "We took everything we loved from various fighting games we grew up with and mashed them all together... then we sprinkled in some RPG elements, like stat boosting and leveling up, so the player can feel more powerful as they progress through the story." The inputs stay simple — "pretty much anyone can pull off any move they choose to" (Smash/FighterZ-style direction+button) — while each character's *personality shines through their animations*, so styles feel unique without being hard to learn. Impact is engineered deliberately: "a deadly cocktail of hit stop, just the right amount of screen shake, big loud VFX, crunchy sound effects, and the animations themselves." Tierney credits long-term retention to players still streaming the game years later and to an ever-expanding shared world: "one of the big appeals about doing River City games in general was always that we would get to reuse (and expand on) this massive city with each game." Combat iteration loop: Rudis watches Twitch streams as "my own personal feedback about what did and didn't work" and reworks the sequel accordingly.
- Key quotes:
  - "We took everything we loved from various fighting games we grew up with and mashed them all together for this title. Then we sprinkled in some RPG elements, like stat boosting and leveling up, so the player can feel more powerful as they progress through the story." — Bannon Rudis, Assistant Director/Combat Designer, RPGamer interview (2019)
  - "It's a deadly cocktail of hit stop, just the right amount of screen shake, big loud VFX, crunchy sound effects, and the animations themselves. The smears and blurs we add to skip frames really make moves have a lot of weight and power behind them." — Bannon Rudis, Siliconera interview (2021)
  - "Each fighting style should feel different enough from each other but not so foreign that a player wouldn't be able to understand a new character right away... it's the fact that their personalities shine through their animations that make them feel so unique from each other." — Bannon Rudis, Siliconera interview (2021)
  - "I actively watch River City Girls streams on Twitch and watch how players are actually playing the game without guidance. I use these streams as my own personal feedback about what did and didn't work as well as I hoped for in the first one." — Bannon Rudis, Siliconera interview (2021)
- Why players love/support it: Kyoko and Misako's charismatic lead-turn (the franchise's first female-led mainline entry), recruiting defeated enemies as allies, the anime presentation and Megan McDuffee soundtrack, 6 playable characters in RCG2 with distinct styles, dojo-purchased moves, and an expanding shared city that rewards returning players.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Premium game + sequel model; no on-record statements about a live-service or MTX philosophy. Tierney has described holding major characters back (Sabu, Ken, Ryuji, Ryuichi) so a potential sequel "wouldn't feel staffed with just minor characters" (ComicBook, ~2024) — monetization via sequels/expansions, not extraction.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): RPG-style growth inside a brawler (leveling, dojo-bought moves, recruitable allies) gives players a reason to come back to the *same world* every day. And watch your players play — stream VODs are free QA that tells you what actually works, better than any internal test.
