# Warframe — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Digital Extremes
- Genre / platforms: Free-to-play co-op action shooter / PC, PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo Switch, mobile
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): "Unabashed fearlessness to mix things up" — live operations producer Rebecca Ford's formula: the game is an ever-evolving experience with hands-on devs who are "constantly aware that if we're getting bored of something, players are too." Combine constant evolution with radical transparency: "We show the raw stuff, things that a lot of people would be embarrassed to show, to get a gauge for how they players are feeling" (creative director Geoff Crookes), and players "will tell them" when something's wrong. Cinematic story twists (The Second Dream, The New War) reactivated huge numbers of accounts — Crookes: "We saw a lot of players come back... That motivated us, excited us and got us to invest in that kind of worldbuilding." Underneath it: movement and combat that feel so good the grind rarely feels like work.
- Key quotes:
  - "That's the key for us to keep players interested: an unabashed fearlessness to mix things up if we feel like it's needed. Warframe is an ever-evolving experience, so there's always something new to try or do. We as devs have our hands on the game every single day, so we're constantly aware that if we're getting bored of something, players are too." — Rebecca Ford, Live Operations/Community Producer (PC Gamer, 2016)
  - "We show the raw stuff, things that a lot of people would be embarrassed to show, to get a gauge for how they players are feeling about the changes, to get a feeling if we're on the right path or if we need to massage things a little bit." — Geoff Crookes, Creative Director (Polygon, 2018)
  - "We're this massive, ridiculous studio, but it feels like we're a rock band. We get the feedback from the fans because you go on stage and you see how people react to something, and you want to make more stuff for the audience to have that interaction." — Steve Sinclair, former Creative Director (Polygon, 2018)
  - "We saw a lot of accounts get reactivated when The Second Dream started getting the word out. That motivated us, excited us and got us to invest in that kind of worldbuilding." — Geoff Crookes, Creative Director (PC Gamer 10-year retrospective, 2023)
- Why players love/support it: Developers who play alongside the community, change things when streams show players hate them (DE has changed a Warframe's powerset after a bad stream reaction), ethical free-to-play design, and story twists that genuinely reframe the whole game.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): The "ethical free-to-play economy" (Polygon): focus progression on "meaningful forward motion during every session, while leaning away from random elements like loot boxes or weapon drops" — "If you want something bad enough, you can find the path to earning it." Trust keeps players coming back, but even that "wouldn't be enough without a monetization and progression system" that respects the player.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Be fearless about mixing things up (stale = death), but only alongside radical transparency — show the raw work, stream it, change course on live player reaction; make monetization earnable-first so spending feels like support, not ransom.
