# PUBG: Battlegrounds — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Krafton (PUBG Corp; originally Bluehole)
- Genre / platforms: Battle royale shooter; PC, consoles, mobile (free-to-play, 2017)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Every single match is genuinely different — random loot, player-driven drops, shrinking zones mean "a unique experience every single time they play a match"; everyone starts equal, so even a frying-pan loadout can win "if you played your cards right"; the game never prescribes one way to play — players get the choice to play however they want; and PUBG is a platform, not just a game: custom modes, modding tools, and a deliberate "find the next PlayerUnknown" philosophy let the community extend the game forever.
- Key quotes:
  - "I think it's because every match is different... You could have a frying pan and possibly win if you played your cards right. It's about giving players a unique experience every single time they play a match." — Brendan Greene ("PlayerUnknown"), Creative Director, Bluehole/PUBG Corp, GamesIndustry.biz (2017)
  - "Everyone has the same chance. There's a little bit of luck involved, but if you're smart, you can survive to the end most times. It's giving players the choice to play however they want. For me, that's what I've always wanted. I don't want to restrict people and tell them this is the way you have to play our game." — Brendan Greene ("PlayerUnknown"), Creative Director, Bluehole/PUBG Corp, GamesIndustry.biz (2017)
  - "I want to find the next PlayerUnknown, I want to find that person who creates something using my game that lets them go on to create their own game." — Brendan Greene ("PlayerUnknown"), Creative Director, Bluehole/PUBG Corp, GamesIndustry.biz (2017)
  - "It's important to give Twitch streamers what they want, but you still have to make your game your way at the end of the day." — Brendan Greene ("PlayerUnknown"), Creative Director, Bluehole/PUBG Corp, GamesIndustry.biz (2017)
- Why players love/support it: Defined the entire battle royale genre; the "winner winner chicken dinner" rush is a cultural touchstone; squad play turned it into a hangout game where friendships form and old ones reconnect (Greene cites players reconnecting with high-school friends as what he's proudest of); still a top Steam concurrent years later.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): "I just want to make a good game... Monetization, that will come later, when the game's stable. Our priority is getting a good experience for everyone." [unverified] — Brendan Greene, Creative Director, Digital Trends (2017; search snippet only). On-record philosophy = game quality first, monetization only after the game is stable.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Every session must be genuinely different — randomize loot, drop points, and endgame conditions so no two runs feel alike, and never dictate one "correct" playstyle. Then hand the game to players: custom modes and modding tools turn your community into your content team — that's what makes a game last a decade.
