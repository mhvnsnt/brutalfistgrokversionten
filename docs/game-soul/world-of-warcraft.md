# World of Warcraft — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Blizzard Entertainment
- Genre / platforms: MMORPG / PC
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): A rich world plus community. Game director Ion Hazzikostas says WoW's longevity is "a mix of a rich world" with moral gray areas (noble Horde, flawed Alliance), the chance for players to "carve their own paths," and — above all — the friendships formed in Azeroth: "The greatest strength of WoW is the community, the friendships, the bonds fans have created in Azeroth through playing the game together. They stand to this day." On the design side: retain the core identity (executive producer Holly Longdale's "coming home" philosophy — don't "zag" so hard it feels unfamiliar) while serving different demographics in parallel (five live versions of the game at once, plus the new WoW: Forever line with no expiration date). Blizzard president Johanna Faries calls it "a never-ending chase to strike the right balance" between fresh and familiar across demographics "that only have so much time in their day."
- Key quotes:
  - "The greatest strength of WoW is the community, the friendships, the bonds fans have created in Azeroth through playing the game together. They stand to this day." — Ion Hazzikostas, Game Director (Men's Journal, 2026)
  - "There's something really powerful about coming home. This fundamental idea we have is that if you haven't played in six, seven, or 15 years, we want it to be the place you remember and the place you fell in love with." — Holly Longdale, Executive Producer (GameSpot 20th anniversary interview, 2024)
  - "There's no expiration date here, and that's why we called it what we did." — Ion Hazzikostas, Game Director, on World of Warcraft: Forever (Men's Journal, 2026)
  - "You can't have every expansion being such a 'zag' that it feels totally unfamiliar to your core fanbase. It's a never-ending chase to strike the right balance." — Johanna Faries, Blizzard President (Windows Central via GamesRadar, 2024)
- Why players love/support it: Two decades of shared history and friendships; Blizzard's deliberate "keep it familiar" posture means veterans can return after years and still recognize home; multiple flavors (Classic/Hardcore/Retail/Forever) serve different playstyles instead of forcing one evolution.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): No on-record statement found in the sources reviewed beyond Longdale defending the expansion model: expansions are "huge storytelling moments... a really good moment for players to come back and rejoin," and "as far as the business model, we talk about it all the time, but for now, this works for us."
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Protect "coming home" — never change the core identity so much that returning players feel lost; then serve parallel audiences (hardcore, casual, nostalgic) with parallel modes instead of one forced evolution; community bonds are the real retention engine, so build systems (guilds, groups, social content) that forge them.
