# Super Mario Odyssey — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Nintendo EPD (Director: Kenta Motokura; Producer: Yoshiaki Koizumi)
- Genre / platforms: 3D action-platformer; Nintendo Switch (2017)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): The entire design was built around one keyword: SURPRISE. Koizumi challenged Motokura to surprise people, and the team turned it into the game's theme — capture mechanics, hidden Power Moons everywhere, dense sandbox kingdoms where "almost everything that draws the player's attention could potentially reward them." The retention thesis: players return to what delights them unexpectedly, and to a character (Mario) they find easy to get along with. Emotional resonance was designed deliberately — objects telegraph feeling intuitively (spiky = pain, shiny = want) so the game connects across "a lot of different kinds of people."
- Key quotes:
  - "When we first began development, Mr. Koizumi [Yoshiaki Koizumi, the producer of Super Mario Odyssey] gave me some very specific directions. He said, 'I want you to surprise people.' And so we actually took that as one of our themes. Surprise became a keyword as we were thinking about the design of the game." — Kenta Motokura, Director, CNET interview (reported via NintendoEverything), 2017
  - "If an object is spiky, you're communicating that it would be painful to the touch and would hurt Mario. If something is shiny, you're communicating that this is something they would want to pick up. We want the object design and the gameplay to be something that instantly and intuitively resonates emotionally for a lot of different kinds of people." — Kenta Motokura, Director, CNET interview (reported via NintendoEverything), 2017
  - "There are a lot of different ways to view a character like Mario. For us, Mario is really a vehicle to explore new challenges in game design and find new ways to keep players engaged. But from the point of view of players, he's really seen as this bright presence, a character that's very easy to get along with and identify with." — Yoshiaki Koizumi, Producer, CNET interview (reported via NintendoEverything), 2017
  - "We started the project by taking Super Mario's theme of surprise and simply riffing on that to come up with things that were purely fun. The result of that was, the team just came up with a seemingly endless stream of fun prototypes. We wanted to use all of them, and so we thought sandbox spaces would be the best way to play all of those fun ideas." — Kenta Motokura, Director (reported via NintendoEverything), 2017
- Why players love/support it: Power Moons hidden literally everywhere reward curiosity — players keep exploring "just one more corner"; capture mechanic turns every enemy into a new toy; the 2D throwback segments and Pauline's musical festival delivered the franchise's signature joy; post-game moons and the Darker Side gave completionists a brutal-but-fair mountain to climb.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): No on-record monetization philosophy found. Shipped as a complete single-purchase game; retention was driven by surprise density, not spend.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Make SURPRISE a design keyword — density of delightful, unexpected rewards per square meter beats map size for retention. Concrete Dragon's "oh damn what's next" escalation is exactly this philosophy; design every mission like a sandboxes of surprises.
