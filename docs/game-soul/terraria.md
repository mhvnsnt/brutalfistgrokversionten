# Terraria — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Re-Logic (Andrew "Redigit" Spinks, president; Whitney Spinks, VP) — small fully-remote team.
- Genre / platforms: 2D sandbox action-adventure; launched 2011, now on virtually every platform (20.5M+ units at time of interview, now far more).
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Endless free updates keep growing the game (250 items at launch → 3,800 items, 16 bosses, expert mode six years later), so the game sells faster years after launch than at release. Players come back because the game they love keeps getting bigger, and they spread the word, buy extra copies, and create content with it — that community loop is the growth engine.
- Key quotes:
  - "That would be our reputation and the unwavering good will that we have built with our amazing fans" — Andrew Spinks, President, GamesIndustry.biz, 2017 (on what he considers Re-Logic's most valuable asset, ahead of Terraria itself)
  - "Primarily, this speaks to our core philosophy of games and gamers first. ... Deep down, it just feels to us like the right thing to do." — Andrew Spinks, President, GamesIndustry.biz, 2017
  - "Business-wise, we truly feel that this will yield long term support for our company, which will allow us to make awesome games for a very long time — even if we didn't make every single dollar that we could have along the way." — Andrew Spinks, President, GamesIndustry.biz, 2017
  - "We support them by adding to the game that they love to play, and they support us by spreading the word, sometimes buying more than one copy, showcasing more amazing potential in the game with the content that they create, and just by being awesome." — Whitney Spinks, VP, GamesIndustry.biz, 2017
- Why players love/support it: A $9.99 game that has received a decade-plus of free mega-updates (1.3 alone could have earned "several million dollars" as paid DLC); a tiny remote team that treats reputation as its most valuable asset and stayed independent; merch and ports done with creative control.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Games and gamers first; never maximize money at the expense of customers; leaving money on the table deliberately to build long-term goodwill. They openly admit charging $1 for the 1.3 update would have made millions, and chose not to. "We have never been all about maximizing the money at the expense of customers."
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): A reputation for never nickel-and-diming players becomes your most valuable asset — it buys word-of-mouth that paid marketing can't. Keep the team small and remote to avoid overhead pressure that forces monetization decisions harmful to the game's reputation.
