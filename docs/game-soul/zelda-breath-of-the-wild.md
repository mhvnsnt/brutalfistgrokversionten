# The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Nintendo EPD
- Genre / platforms: Open-air action-adventure; Switch, Wii U (2017)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Series producer Eiji Aonuma says the guiding principle was to return to the liberation of the very first Zelda: "one basic concept that I had in mind is that it's okay and fun to get lost." The team spent the first two years of development making "just running around fun, just climbing fun, just riding a horse fun" — the journey itself is the game, and every destination the player sets for themselves leads to "something they've created with a path they've chosen." Link himself was intentionally made near-expressionless: "We wanted the players to be able to relate to Link and to play as themselves," so the world belongs to the player, not to the avatar. Miyamoto frames the essence as: solve problems yourself, explore a crafted landscape, and grow — "Thinking about it yourself and solving it yourself is something essential." Players keep returning because the world's consistent physics systems keep producing outcomes the devs themselves never scripted, and because 100 players experience 100 different journeys ("open-air" freed the team from the blockages of linear design).
- Key quotes:
  - "One basic concept that I had in mind is that it's okay and fun to get lost... With Breath of the Wild, I wanted to basically have the players experience this 'getting lost' while getting to the next destination, because this journey leads to something they've created with a path they've chosen." — Eiji Aonuma, Series Producer, ZackScottGames interview (transcribed by Zelda Universe), 2017
  - "In the first two years of development we tried to create a game where just running around is fun, just climbing is fun, just riding a horse without any scene or loading changes is fun, and we spent obviously a lot of time doing that." — Shigeru Miyamoto, Creator, ZackScottGames interview, 2017
  - "The Link from Breath of the Wild doesn't have much of a character, and that was done intentionally. We wanted the players to be able to relate to Link and to play as themselves." — Eiji Aonuma, Series Producer, ZackScottGames interview, 2017
  - "Thinking about it yourself and solving it yourself is something essential... as long as these three things are kept in balance, I think that's what Zelda is." — Shigeru Miyamoto, Creator, ZackScottGames interview, 2017 (on solve-it-yourself problem solving, exploration, and growth as the Zelda essence)
- Why players love/support it: Total freedom — go anywhere, climb anything, approach everything your way; a world dense with systems that reward curiosity and experimentation; a quiet, melancholic beauty; and the feeling that the adventure genuinely belongs to the player, not a checklist.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): No on-record statement found. ANALYSIS: Nintendo sold BOTW as a complete game at full price (plus a modest Expansion Pass adding two DLC packs); no microtransactions. The return engine is the world's density and replayability, not purchases.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Make movement itself fun — running, climbing, roaming — before worrying about content density; then give players a world consistent enough that they can solve problems their own way, and protect getting lost as a feature, not a bug.
