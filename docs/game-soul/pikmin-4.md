# Pikmin 4 — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Nintendo EPD (Producer: Shigeru Miyamoto; Chief Director: Yuji Kando; Planning Directors: Yutaka Hiramuki, Taku Matoba)
- Genre / platforms: Real-time strategy / action-adventure; Nintendo Switch (2023)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): The franchise's unique hook is its relationship with mortality — "If something is irreversible, you need to figure out a way to prevent undesired things from happening," and that planning instinct is "Dandori" (thinking about planning and efficiency in advance). Miyamoto says that IS what makes the game unique. Kando adds that task-management gives "a sense of accomplishment that even people who don't play games can relate to — like when you're doing housework or cooking." Pikmin 4's whole design was "let the fun of Dandori sink in naturally" — lower the entry barrier (controls, camera, Oatchi the space-dog helper, rewind feature) while keeping the planning depth, and sneak training into the story via Dandori Challenges and Dandori Battles.
- Key quotes:
  - "I get that people find it more difficult when death is a factor. But I think the franchise's strength lies in its relationship with mortality. If something is irreversible, you need to figure out a way to prevent undesired things from happening. To try to prevent Pikmin from dying, you need to practise 'Dandori'. To me, that's what makes this game unique." — Shigeru Miyamoto, Producer, Nintendo "Ask the Developer, Vol. 10: Pikmin 4 – Chapter 2", 2023
  - "We realised that task management games give a sense of accomplishment that even people who don't play games can relate to. It's like when you're doing housework or cooking. Once you get used to it, you start to think about Dandori and then you get things done more efficiently. I think that process is fun." — Yuji Kando, Chief Director, Nintendo "Ask the Developer, Vol. 10: Pikmin 4 – Chapter 1", 2023
  - "We wanted players to enjoy the game in greater depth and for longer by offering those Dandori elements, while removing the time limit. So basically, Pikmin 4 gets all the good things from the first and second titles." — Yuji Kando, Chief Director, Nintendo "Ask the Developer, Vol. 10: Pikmin 4 – Chapter 2", 2023
  - "A good idea is something which solves multiple issues at once." — Shigeru Miyamoto, Producer, Nintendo "Ask the Developer, Vol. 10: Pikmin 4 – Chapter 2", 2023
- Why players love/support it: Pikmin 4 became the best-selling Pikmin game — the accessibility push (rewind, Oatchi, co-op) removed the fear of losing Pikmin while preserving the deep planning; night expeditions and Dandori Battles gave veterans new systems; cute-but-real stakes create genuine attachment; every playthrough can be optimized, inviting replays.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): No on-record monetization philosophy found; shipped as a complete single-purchase game with no DLC monetization in the traditional sense.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Build a "Dandori" core — a skill loop players can visibly get better at across replays (planning, efficiency, mastery) — and ease newcomers in by making the fun sink in naturally rather than dumbing down the depth. A good design idea solves multiple problems at once (Miyamoto's test).
