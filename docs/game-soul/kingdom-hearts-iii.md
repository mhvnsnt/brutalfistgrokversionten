# Kingdom Hearts III — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Square Enix (Business Division 3; directed by Tetsuya Nomura; co-developed with Disney/Pixar collaboration on worlds)
- Genre / platforms: Action RPG; PS4/Xbox One (2019), PC (2021), Switch cloud versions
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): The players THEMSELVES are the engine of longevity — "What I remember is not so much the impact that these games had on the fans around the world, but the one that the players had on the series itself and that is the reason for its longevity." The game is built as a playground: "one of the essential elements that make an interesting title is the fact of making it a playground. Without granting a certain freedom to the players, this is not possible, so we did to include as many choices as possible." Sora is deliberately "ordinary" so every player sees themselves: "even if you're 'ordinary', everyone can exhibit a special power just like Sora, as long as it's for something important."
- Key quotes: (2-4; each: "quote" — Name, Role, Publication/Event, Year; append [unverified] if from a search snippet not a fetched page)
  - "What I remember is not so much the impact that these games had on the fans around the world, but the one that the players had on the series itself and that is the reason for its longevity. I am very grateful to them for allowing the series to last so long." — Tetsuya Nomura, Director, Gameblog (Gamescom 2018, via kh13)
  - "One of the essential elements that make an interesting title is the fact of making it a playground. Without granting a certain freedom to the players, this is not possible, so we did to include as many choices as possible." — Tetsuya Nomura, Director, Gameblog (Gamescom 2018, via kh13)
  - "Kingdom Hearts III is truly a game suitable for both types of players. And for those who discover the saga with this episode, I would be delighted if it gave them the opportunity to take an interest in the series and also immerse themselves in the other episodes." — Tetsuya Nomura, Director, Gameblog (Gamescom 2018, via kh13)
  - "I had the premise that a heart like Sora's exists within all the players. Sora is 'ordinary', therefore everyone is 'ordinary'. I figure even if you're 'ordinary', everyone can exhibit a special power just like Sora, as long as it's for something important." — Tetsuya Nomura, Director, KH3 Ultimania/archive interviews (via GameFAQs fan archive) [unverified]
- Why players love/support it: A 16-year saga conclusion built for both newcomers (memory archive recaps) and day-one fans; Disney worlds (Toy Story, Frozen, Pirates) pull in new audiences while the Xehanort-saga resolution rewards the faithful; friendship depicted with "a sense of reality" (Nomura, IGN) — childhood bonds changing as you grow — lands the theme.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Full-price premium release; "Re Mind" story DLC as the upsell. Devs credit longevity to the player community, not to monetization — "gorge the game of content to satisfy the fans of the first hour."
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Credit the players as the reason the thing survives ("the players had on the series itself... that is the reason for its longevity"), design as a playground of choices, and make the hero deliberately "ordinary" so players project themselves onto them.
