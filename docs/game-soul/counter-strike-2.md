# Counter-Strike 2 — Heart and Soul

- Developer / studio: Valve (originally a Half-Life mod by Minh "Gooseman" Le and Jess "Cliffe" Cliffe, acquired by Valve)
- Genre / platforms: Tactical 5v5 FPS / PC

- Why players come back (in the devs' view): An unchanging, trusted competitive core — Valve deliberately refuses to redesign shooting fundamentals ("we don't have any plans to change the basics"), betting on the deep mastery curve and the matchmaking/economy systems players have invested thousands of hours into. Longevity comes from listening to the community ("Players, players, and players"), bringing player inventories forward between titles, and treating development as an ongoing process rather than a fixed vision.

- Key quotes:
  - "What are the three important things every Counter-Strike map should have? Players, players, and players. Every great Counter-Strike map started off as a not-great Counter-Strike map, and the difference has always been player feedback." — CS2 Dev Team, PC Gamer, 2023
  - "What problem does aiming downsights solve, and do we have that problem? ...it's not something our players are expressing any concern about. So we don't have any plans to change the basics in CS2." — CS2 Dev Team, on refusing to redesign shooting fundamentals, PC Gamer, 2023
  - "Bringing the items forward is just the customer-centered way to bridge between a game and its successor." — CS2 Dev Team, on carrying CS:GO inventories into CS2, PC Gamer, 2023
  - "We don't have a specific vision that we're trying to realize, but rather a process we want to continue to execute for many years." — CS2 Dev Team, Dot Esports, 2023 [unverified]

- Why players love/support it: Trust — their skills, ranks, and inventories carry forward across two decades of the franchise; the game never invalidates mastery with gimmicky redesigns; the player-driven skin economy and community maps (surf, workshop) make players co-owners of the game.

- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): No on-record statement found from the CS2 team about monetization philosophy directly; the team's public stance is cosmetic-only (weapon skins, stickers, cases) — all gameplay weapons free, no pay-to-win. Valve's general microtransaction doctrine applies: see Dota 2 file (Kyle Davis "regret test"). CS2 monetization specifically: No on-record statement found.

- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Never wipe player investment — carry progress, items, and mastery forward between builds/versions. A rock-solid competitive core that players trust not to be redesigned on a whim is the retention engine; experiments belong in modes around the core, never in the core itself.
