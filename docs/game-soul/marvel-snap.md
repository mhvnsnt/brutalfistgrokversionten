# Marvel Snap — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Second Dinner (Nuverse / Skystone Games publisher)
- Genre / platforms: Digital collectible card game; iOS, Android, PC (Steam)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): No two matches ever feel the same. Second Dinner's design pillars, per co-founder Ben Brode: "rough edges" (variance from wild locations that create emotional variety and skill-testing adaptation), "wow" cards that blow minds and signal "anything could happen in this game" (Agatha Harkness playing your hand for you), and the snap mechanic that turns every game into a pressure-cooker bluffing/bail decision. Fast matches + constant novelty = one more game.
- Key quotes:
  - "We can go too far [with polish]. We can over-polish and then everything feels the same, right? And locations like Ego, District X, and Subterranea... add a huge amount of texture to the game... it does change the experience and adds some emotional variance to playing the game, and I think that matters." — Ben Brode, Chief Development Officer / co-founder, Second Dinner, Twinfinite interview, 2023
  - "I think those locations are actually quite skill-testing!... when you are forced into a new situation that you've never been in before, the player who is better at adapting on the fly and puzzling out a situation is going to do better in that situation." — Ben Brode, CDO, Second Dinner, Twinfinite interview, 2023
  - "You need something that blows their mind and makes them think: 'Hold on! Anything could happen in this game! I don't know what to expect! What else do these cards do? I have to see all of these cards now! This is wild!'" — Ben Brode, CDO, Second Dinner, Eurogamer interview, 2023
  - "If you're not good at determining whether to snap, you should probably be snapping round one, because it's better than never snapping!" — Ben Brode, CDO, Second Dinner, Kotaku interview, 2023 [unverified]
- Why players love/support it: 3-6 minute games with no mana flood/screw, no waiting through a dead opponent's turn; every match is a fresh puzzle because locations and cards collide unpredictably; "great stories" (Brode's words) — District X giving both players random decks, Ego taking over, a Subterranea bluff stealing cubes. The card art/variants culture turns collection itself into an art hobby. ANALYSIS: Players consistently cite generosity of the free core game as why they *want* to spend — a "thank you" purchase, not a tax.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): No dev quote on pricing philosophy was found on the record. ANALYSIS: In practice, Marvel Snap monetizes via the Season Pass (Battle Pass equivalent), cosmetic variants, and bundles — no card can be bought directly for money in most cases, and the "snap/retreat" design means losing costs almost nothing, so spending feels optional rather than coerced. Players report paying "because I want to say thank you to these people who have given me so much joy."
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Build in "rough edges" and wild variance that generate player stories — Concrete Dragon's street fights could use surprise-territory/location twists that create memorable moments players retell. Monetize cosmetics/variants players buy as a thank-you, not power gates that feel like rent.
