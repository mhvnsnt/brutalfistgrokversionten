# Fate/Grand Order — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: TYPE-MOON (Kinoko Nasu, scenario/director), developed by DelightWorks (creative director Yosuke Shiokawa), published by Aniplex. (FGO Project)
- Genre / platforms: Turn-based RPG / visual-novel hybrid with gacha summoning; iOS + Android (2015 JP, 2017 EN; one of the highest-grossing mobile games ever).
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Story and characters first — players return for the unfolding grand narrative and the Servants they love, not systems. The devs deliberately engineer shared "real-time" moments (raid battles, simultaneous final-chapter events) so the whole player base experiences climaxes together, turning individual play into a collective event.
- Key quotes:
  - "I wanted everyone to work hard and bring over the future – the next year – with their own hands." — Kinoko Nasu, scenario writer/director (TYPE-MOON), 4Gamer interview (translated), March 2017
  - "The development team managed to understand that as well – the feeling of putting the heart of the story first before commercial reasons. I think FGO is a blessed game in that aspect." — Kinoko Nasu, 4Gamer interview (translated), March 2017
  - "I think that's part of the charm of being an administration-type game. We managed to link the player unity from challenging the raid battles to the excitement as the story moves towards the climax." — Yosuke Shiokawa, creative director (DelightWorks), 4Gamer interview (translated), March 2017
  - "This kind of event is not possible without, to say the least, the players of this game... we wanted to show our gratitude towards the fans... what we'd call 'difficult' is that we were able to obtain and retain this amount of players from the development side." — Yosuke Shiokawa, RPG Site (FGO USA Tour 2019 interview), 2019
- Why players love/support it: Servants are beloved franchise/legend characters with distinct writing and art; the game monetizes love for characters (gacha to "fight alongside and flirt with" favorites, per press); players who stay past Part 1 genuinely enjoy reading the story (Nasu: "players who have come as far as past the conclusion of part 1 of the game probably enjoy reading"). Community rituals (cosplay, anniversary events, shared raids) reinforce belonging. ANALYSIS (press/community): no stamina paywalls on progression items, no PvP arms race, and old characters get buffed — generosity that builds trust and long-term spend willingness.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): No on-record statement found from Nasu/Shiokawa explicitly describing the gacha pricing philosophy. Closest on-record: the team ethos is "the heart of the story first before commercial reasons" (Nasu, 2017). The model is gacha-only — you cannot buy power items, stamina, or inventory expansion; spending is entirely about characters.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Story and character love is the retention engine; systems are the stage. Build synchronized, everyone-at-once story events (season finales, boss raids) that players experience together — shared moments create the community that spends.
