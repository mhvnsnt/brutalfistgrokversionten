# Streets of Rage 4 — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Dotemu (publisher/design) + Lizardcube (art) + Guard Crush Games (gameplay) — lead designer Jordi Asensio, executive producer Cyrille Imbert, art director Ben Fiquet, tech director Cyrille Lagarigue
- Genre / platforms: Side-scrolling beat-'em-up / PS4, Xbox One, Switch, PC, mobile
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): A manifesto in three words — "something pure, simple, and efficient" (Imbert, PlayStation Blog). The mission was to resurrect a feeling, not a codebase: "it was very important to bring back the *feeling* you had when playing the originals" (Lagarigue, Limited Run Games). Asensio's personal design law: "give people instant fun while creating a deep system that will make people interested in mastering the game and coming back again and again." The retention mechanics are concrete beat-'em-up design wisdom: distinct characters so "every player can find his style of play and express him/herself"; solid 60fps and reactive controls; the special-move health-gambit system (spend health on specials, earn it back by hitting enemies — "opens the door for some aggressive play styles, gambling a lot of life in order to do huge combos"); memorable, limited enemy encounters instead of screen-filling spam so "the player's brain [is] always on"; stage-restart on death instead of game-restart so "you lose less than 10 minutes of progression, and you can learn from your mistakes." Deliberately NOT a competitive game: "The balance needs to be against the computer, not against your friends" (Imbert, PCGamesN).
- Key quotes:
  - "For my part I tried to give people instant fun while creating a deep system that will make people interested in mastering the game and come back again and again." — Jordi Asensio, Lead Game Designer, PlayStation Blog interview (2020)
  - "After more than 25 years, I think it was better to be too close to the original than too modern... It was not just a case of bringing back the characters or the story; it was very important to bring back the *feeling* you had when playing the originals." — Cyrille Lagarigue, Tech Director, Limited Run Games interview (2020)
  - "Being able to play the game in different ways is what feeds the player's imagination so he/she will always come back to the game to try new things." — Jordi Asensio, Nintendo Life interview (2019)
  - "The balance needs to be against the computer, not against your friends. It's not really the point of the game." — Cyrille Imbert, Executive Producer, PCGamesN interview (2019)
- Why players love/support it: The direct conduit to the Genesis originals' feel with modern hand-drawn animation; distinct, expressive character roster (Cherry, Floyd additions); co-op as the core social loop; fan-driven development — "every event we could show a demo at (like PAX) was a gold mine. We changed, added, and balanced considerably thanks to this feedback" (Asensio).
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Premium game + the *Mr. X Nightmare* DLC (survival mode, 3 new characters, new tracks) — a meaty expansion at a low price point. Nothing on record about aggressive monetization; the model is sell-on-quality, premium package, fan-service content.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Beat-'em-up retention = distinct-feeling characters, memorable small encounters, instant fun with deep mastery underneath — and the game is balanced against the *computer*, never friends; co-op is the social engine, not PvP. Test with fans at every event and let them reshape the game.
