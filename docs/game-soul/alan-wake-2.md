# Alan Wake 2 — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Remedy Entertainment (creative director Sam Lake; game director Kyle Rowley)
- Genre / platforms: Survival horror / narrative thriller; PS5, Xbox Series X/S, PC (2023)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): 13 years of sustained fan love. Lake says fan support was "really, really important": the constant "ALAN WAKE 2?" / "ALAN WAKE 2 CONFIRMED" social-media drumbeat plus "heartfelt deeper love" kept the project alive through four failed pitch attempts. Remedy waited until the concept *became* survival horror — "this version" is "by far the darkest" Remedy ever conceived and "we could not have made this game earlier." Game director Kyle Rowley says the team wanted "a more cohesive package" and the pivot to full survival horror (scarce resources, slower considered monster encounters, "every shot counts") was what made the sequel right. Lake personally felt the project was "special" and honest expectation-setting with fans ("Today isn't that day, but I hope one day can be") deepened trust.
- Key quotes: (2-4; each: "quote" — Name, Role, Publication/Event, Year; append [unverified] if from a search snippet not a fetched page)
  - "[Fan support was] really really important. I mean, we have awesome fans and the amount of social media over all the possible channels, the amount of times it has been, 'Alan Wake 2?' or capital letters, 'ALAN WAKE 2 CONFIRMED' as a reaction to anything, pretty much. But also clearly, more heartfelt deeper love shown to us with Alan Wake." — Sam Lake, creative director, IGN (via ComingSoon.net), 2021 [unverified]
  - "This is the first time we've succeeded. We've been trying through the years and the time was not right back then... I am really, really happy that none of them were made, because that meant that this got made now — and we could not have made this game earlier." — Sam Lake, creative director, PCGamesN (via Gameranx), 2023 [unverified]
  - "We worked on multiple versions of the [Alan Wake 2] concept along the way. And what we have now as Alan Wake 2, I'm so happy it's *this* version. I'm so happy we did not get the opportunity to go with the earlier ones." — Sam Lake, creative director, GameSpot, 2023 [unverified]
  - "As a metaphor, you can imagine that, you know, Alan Wake is Remedy. He has had his success... but he is now turning his back on his success — and moving on from our success is going from Max Payne to Alan Wake... this is Remedy breaking out of the bounds of traditional video game storytelling — it's another risk, another unexpected choice." — Sam Lake, creative director (via EssentiallySports), 2023 [unverified]
- Why players love/support it: Remedy's signature layered storytelling (live action, metanarrative, Remedy Connected Universe ties to Control), uncompromising survival-horror pivot, and a studio that visibly listens to fans. Players supported it for 13 years *before* it existed — proof of the compounding value of treating fans as collaborators.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Premium single-purchase + story expansions (Night Springs, The Lake House). No on-record dev monetization philosophy found.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Don't force a sequel/franchise beat until the *right version* exists — killing four good-enough pitches produced the version fans actually wanted. Honest communication ("today isn't that day") earns a decade of patience.
