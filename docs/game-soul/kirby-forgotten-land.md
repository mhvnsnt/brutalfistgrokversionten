# Kirby and the Forgotten Land — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: HAL Laboratory (General Director: Shinya Kumazaki; Director: Tatsuya Kamiyama; Nintendo associate producer: Kei Ninomiya)
- Genre / platforms: 3D action-platformer; Nintendo Switch (2022)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): HAL's design philosophy is "enjoyable for anyone, as well as being highly accessible yet still offering a lot of depth." Kumazaki designed around a 3D platformer "easy enough that even a three-year-old child could play," yet the game hides real depth (evolved Copy Abilities, hidden missions per stage, post-game challenges). Players replay because every stage holds discoverable secrets and because the game is tuned to make players *feel* skilled — if an attack looks like it "should" hit, the game makes it connect. That generosity is the retention loop: players come back to what makes them feel good.
- Key quotes:
  - "The concepts of games being enjoyable for anyone, as well as being highly accessible yet still offering a lot of depth, are aspects that we have treasured in the Kirby series. When I began planning this game's difficulty level, I pictured a 3D platformer easy enough that even a three-year-old child could play..." — Shinya Kumazaki, General Director, Nintendo "Ask the Developer, Vol. 4", 2022
  - "Everyone on our development staff really loves Kirby, so no one wanted to torment him with an unfair situation. We also thought about people who aren't skilled at 3D platformers and wanted to provide them with quieter areas where they could play and explore peacefully." — Shinya Kumazaki, General Director, Nintendo "Ask the Developer, Vol. 4", 2022
  - "Now, if it looks like an attack 'should' hit on-screen, we make sure it does connect — even if it was going to narrowly miss... even people who are not so good at 3D action games can attack enemies without any stress." — Tatsuya Kamiyama, Director, Nintendo "Ask the Developer, Vol. 4", 2022
  - "Personally, I thought that a Kirby game where you could freely explore a 3D space would be a lot of fun... we spent a lot of time thinking about what to do to make a proper mainline Kirby game that can be enjoyed in 3D." — Tatsuya Kamiyama, Director, Nintendo "Ask the Developer, Vol. 4", 2022
- Why players love/support it: First fully-3D mainline Kirby; Mouthful Mode (inhaling a car, vending machine) became a viral delight; copy-ability evolutions and hidden stage missions reward replays; Waddle Dee Town building gives cozy progression; it trusts players of any age while hiding a genuinely demanding post-game for veterans.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): No on-record monetization philosophy found. Shipped as a complete single-purchase game; retention came from design generosity, not spend.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Make every action the player thinks "should" work actually work — generous hit-aim, forgiving landings, intuitive responses — because feeling competent is what brings players back, not difficulty. "Accessible for a three-year-old, deep for veterans" is a design target, not a contradiction.
