# Injustice 2 — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: NetherRealm Studios (creative director Ed Boon; lead designer John Edwards; art director Steve Baron)
- Genre / platforms: Superhero fighting game / PS4, Xbox One, PC (Steam), mobile
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Two pillars — **content and self-expression**. Boon explicitly positions NetherRealm's single-player content as the differentiator: "telling an elaborate story, giving very long missions to someone who plays single player... People appreciate that and we plan on continuing that tradition." The Gear system was designed as the online-retention engine: "You are in a constant process of refining, upgrading, and improving your version of a character," with mirror matches that are never identical ("his one will be different to mine"). Goodman: "It's a way to elevate yourself without having to be the greatest fighting game player out there and make the game much more palatable for a casual audience as well." Story draws from "decades of history" of the DC universe; the team listens on social for roster picks (Red Hood was added on overwhelming fan demand).
- Key quotes:
  - "You are in a constant process of refining, upgrading, and improving your version of a character. And you can have the whole cast levelled up, or just focus on one." — Ed Boon, Creative Director, GameSpot interview (2017)
  - "It's a way to elevate yourself without having to be the greatest fighting game player out there and make the game much more palatable for a casual audience as well. You may not be able to go online and beat 90 per cent of players out there but you can have the coolest-looking Batman. You can show off the fact you have put hundreds of hours into a character." — Brian Goodman, Eurogamer interview (2016)
  - "One of the things we want to have people do is, when you are playing online and you see someone you are fighting against with really cool gear, you say, 'I want that helmet, how do I get that?' And talk with that person. And that would rope people into playing some other game modes and experiencing some other content that maybe they wouldn't have normally gone to." — Steve Baron, Art Director, GameSpot interview (2017)
  - "One of the messages I've been trying to communicate and talk about with this game is exactly that... telling an elaborate story, giving very long missions to someone who plays single player... that was a positive lesson to learn that worked out well. People appreciate that and we plan on continuing that tradition." — Ed Boon, Creative Director, Game Informer interview (2012, Injustice 1; philosophy carried into I2)
- Why players love/support it: The cinematic story mode (the genre template Capcom copied); gear/costume hunting as an endless personal project; the Multiverse's rotating single-player challenges; beloved DC roster with gear that changes how characters play (augmentations, gear sets); tournament mode for the competitive side.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Premium game + DLC fighters; **"there won't be any gear sold as DLC"** (Boon, Twitch livestream via PlayStation LifeStyle, 2017) and "Injustice 2 will never allow you to spend real money to improve your stats" (Edwards, GameSpot, 2017) — cosmetic self-expression is the grind; stat power can't be bought.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Never let players pay for stats; sell them *identity* instead — the grind for "the coolest-looking Batman" keeps people playing longer than leaderboards do. And invest in story/single-player content aggressively: most fighting-game buyers never compete, and content is why they stay.
