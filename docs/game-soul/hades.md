# Hades — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Supergiant Games (Greg Kasavin, writer/designer; Amir Rao, studio director).
- Genre / platforms: Roguelike dungeon crawler; PC + consoles (2020).
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Early access turned the game into a "living world" — monthly major updates added characters and story beats in quasi-real time, like a TV show unfolding; dying advances the story, so failure is progress; a tight feedback loop with players let the team prioritize the right late-game systems and story content, and players' visible excitement "invigorating" the team fueled the next update.
- Key quotes:
  - "As part of our early access we make it feel like it's a living world from us... And you almost start to get the impression that the story of the game takes place kind of in quasi-real time, like here's what happened a month later, and now this guy showed up in the House of Hades who wasn't there before." — Greg Kasavin, Writer/Designer, USgamer/VG247 DICE 2019 interview, 2019
  - "By the numbers everybody who had ever played the game came back. Just as many people were playing it with our first major update as had ever played it up until that point... We proved to ourselves that we have this ability to update the game in a meaningful enough way to where people excitedly come back to it." — Greg Kasavin, Writer/Designer, USgamer/VG247 DICE 2019 interview, 2019
  - "I think we feel a responsibility to our players to keep doing our best and make the best game that we can. It's cool to be able to hear from them right away and refine the decisions that we're making." — Greg Kasavin, Writer/Designer, USgamer/VG247 DICE 2019 interview, 2019
  - "The mindset was pretty simple: What would these characters talk about and what would they notice? Add those interactions. Early Access meant we could hurtle forward. Our players liked the characters for the most part so we kept developing them." — Greg Kasavin, Writer/Designer, @kasavin on X, Sept 24 2020 (via GameSpot)
- Why players love/support it: Dying advances the story instead of resetting it; beloved characters with thousands of contextual interactions; Supergiant's trust bank built over Bastion/Transistor/Pyre; early-access players shaped the game's systems and tone directly. Also: accessibility done right — God Mode buffs damage resistance per death rather than a traditional easy mode, so struggling players still experience the story.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): No on-record statement found on monetization philosophy specifically. Supergiant operates as a small self-funded studio shipping premium complete games; Hades' early access launched with monthly major updates included at no extra cost, and post-1.0 narrative arc had a definitive conclusion with no story DLC model.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Make failure advance the story/progression, not erase it — that turns every run into a reason to come back. Build in the open with a visible update cadence; players who see their feedback land in the game become your marketing.
