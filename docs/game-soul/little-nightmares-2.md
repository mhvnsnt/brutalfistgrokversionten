# Little Nightmares II — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Tarsier Studios; published by Bandai Namco
- Genre / platforms: Cinematic puzzle-platformer / horror adventure; PS4, Xbox One, Switch, PC (2021)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Wordless, interpretive storytelling + a bond with a vulnerable companion. Senior narrative designer Dave Mervik on why Six returned: "I was really keen to go full new character and let people wonder what happened to Six, but people are in love with that character. We knew there was more to tell with her as well." Six as an AI companion (deliberately not co-op — "co-op would just kill that... it would destroy the atmosphere we built up") creates new dynamics: the player can't control Six, so "players are never fully in charge of Six's fate" — and when she's separated, "you feel more alone than you could ever have felt in the original game." Theme: escape — "What do you want to escape from? In our world, why do people feel the need to escape so much into their TV shows and movies, their screens...?" Story is told entirely through environment, animation, and sound design, leaving room for player interpretation; reviewers noted small details only land on replay.

  - "Now you don't have that [control over Six], so this character you're very connected with is a bit more distant from you. I think it introduces a really nice dynamic. How do these two children work together? What’s their story? What’s their relationship and how does it evolve? Do you still feel alone when you’re with someone else?" — Dave Mervik, senior narrative designer, The Escapist, 2020 [unverified]
  - "What do you want to escape from? In our world, why do people feel the need to escape so much into their TV shows and movies, their screens, and into completely different identities?" — Dave Mervik, senior narrative designer, The Escapist, 2020 [unverified]
- Why players love/support it: The haunting art, the wordless lore, the hand-holding button — players bonded to the Mono/Six relationship (compared to Ico by critics). Community theory-crafting over the ending keeps the universe alive; Bandai Namco saw enough in the IP to continue it beyond Tarsier.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Premium single-purchase; no on-record dev monetization philosophy found.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): A companion you *can't fully control* creates more tension and attachment than a co-op buddy — protect characters the player loves by keeping them slightly out of reach. And wordless environmental storytelling invites replay to catch details dialogue would have spoiled.
