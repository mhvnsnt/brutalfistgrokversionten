# Guild Wars 2 — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: ArenaNet (co-founder/president Mike O'Brien; designer Colin Johanson)
- Genre / platforms: Fantasy MMORPG, PC (buy-to-play, no subscription; Steam since 2022)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Horizontal progression — the level cap NEVER goes up, so your investment is never invalidated and there's no treadmill to fall off; seeing another player is always good news (shared tags, shared loot); and a living-world content stream where expansions are "punctuation" on an always-growing game. O'Brien: players should "get to the fun part of the game as soon as they sit down" — fun in half an hour, not grind for rewards.
- Key quotes:
  - "We love the fact that Guild Wars 2 is not a treadmill game. We really tried to distance ourselves from MMOs for a long time, because so much of it is — you get in, and you're just on this treadmill. You know the developer is going to just keep adding new runway in front of you. You're never really gonna get to the end." — Mike O'Brien, Co-founder/President & Game Director, Polygon, 2017
  - "We really wanted to make a game where any time you see another player, you're happy to see them." — Mike O'Brien, Co-founder/President & Game Director, Polygon, 2017
  - "Players should be able to get to the fun part of the game as soon as they sit down and play. Let's give players something meaningful in half an hour." — Mike O'Brien, Co-founder/President, GameReactor (GW2 Manifesto interview), 2012 [unverified]
  - "Expansion packs are like punctuation... We're shipping content all the time. Hopefully it all flows together, live releases and expansion packs. But sometimes you want to accomplish something big, and you're going to accomplish it in an expansion pack. You can't really release it piecemeal." — Mike O'Brien, Co-founder/President & Game Director, Polygon, 2017
- Why players love/support it: The "least greedy Western MMO" reputation — buy once, no sub, and a decade of support without a gear treadmill; one of the friendliest MMO communities (the systems engineered cooperation); and a cadence of Living World seasons + expansions that keep the world growing without resetting anyone.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): "Here's our philosophy on microtransactions: We think players should have the opportunity to spend money on items that provide visual distinction and offer more ways to express themselves. They should also be able to spend money on account services and on time-saving convenience items. But it's never OK for players to buy a game and not be able to enjoy what they paid for without additional purchases, and it's never OK for players who spend money to have an unfair advantage over players who spend time." — Mike O'Brien, Co-founder/President, VG247 (ArenaNet blog reprint), 2012. Plus the player-to-player gold↔gem exchange, modeled on EVE's PLEX: "If a player buys gold from another player, he gets the gold he wants, the selling player gets gems she can use for microtransactions, and ArenaNet generates revenue that we can use to keep supporting and updating the game. Everyone wins." (PC Gamer, 2012) [unverified]
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Never invalidate player investment — horizontal progression (new tools, not bigger numbers) lets players leave for a year and return without feeling punished. And write the monetization rule as a hard line: no selling advantage, ever.
