# Animal Crossing: New Horizons — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Nintendo EPD (Animal Crossing team)
- Genre / platforms: Social life simulation; Nintendo Switch (2020)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Director Aya Kyogoku says one pillar of the series is "that we want different kinds of people to enjoy the game no matter their age group, and that they can enjoy it in their own way." Players return because the game is a common language and a part of their real lives: Nintendo seeks "to make Animal Crossing a sort of common language, a starting point for communication between a large variety of people," and Kyogoku notes "how we live in Animal Crossing is also a part of the life and world of each player." Daily routines tied to the real-time clock, seasonal events, terraforming, and crafting give the island something new on every visit, and the game deliberately avoids pressure — Tom Nook charges no interest and waits forever to be repaid. Kyogoku also stresses respecting players' long histories: with each cycle they want the latest entry "the best game possible," while preserving "the memories and affection" players have of older games.
- Key quotes:
  - "Our goal with Animal Crossing is that, at least, the players can be who they want and enjoy the games how they like." — Aya Kyogoku, Director, La Vanguardia interview, 2020 (via ResetEra translation)
  - "We seek to make Animal Crossing a sort of common language, a starting point for communication between a large variety of people... We can say how we live in Animal Crossing is also a part of the life and world of each player." — Aya Kyogoku, Director, 20 Minutes interview, 2020 (via NintendoEverything translation)
  - "With each development [cycle], we always want to ensure that the most recent entry is the best game possible... We don't want to ruin the memories and affection they have for the older games." — Aya Kyogoku, Director, 20 Minutes interview, 2020 (via NintendoEverything translation)
- Why players love/support it: A gentle, pressure-free daily ritual; an island that is truly theirs to shape (terraforming, decorating, crafting); villagers and seasonal events that make the world feel alive; a game that became a social space — players visit each other's islands and share their lives.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): No on-record statement found. ANALYSIS: New Horizons shipped complete at full price with free seasonal updates and one paid DLC (Happy Home Paradise). The retention engine is the daily-routine loop and creative ownership, not purchased consumables.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Give players a place that feels like theirs and rewards daily check-ins with something new — low-pressure routines plus deep self-expression keep people returning for years without any pay-to-win pressure.
