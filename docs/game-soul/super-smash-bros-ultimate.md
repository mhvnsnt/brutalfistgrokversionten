# Super Smash Bros. Ultimate — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Bandai Namco Studios (series created by HAL Laboratory); series director Masahiro Sakurai
- Genre / platforms: Platform fighter / party brawler; Nintendo Switch (2018)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Director Masahiro Sakurai's north star is that Smash is fun for everyone, in every way: "my only hope is that the game reaches all kinds of people... I did my best to implement stages and items to that end. I'm of the opinion that there's no limit to how you should play." That means deliberately hiding win/loss pressure online ("I'd rather you didn't think too hard about how many wins and losses you have... I don't want people to come to feel that way" about quitting), embracing silliness as the point ("Hitting the Fake Smash Ball and having a good laugh is what Smash Bros. is all about"), and refusing to sand characters into sameness for balance's sake — "To make things fair, you have to standardize many things. And when you do, every character ends up feeling the same." The "Everyone Is Here" roster was a deliberate miracle of player service: all 89 fighters included so nobody's favorite was cut. And Smash's identity is feel, not mechanics: "If you got rid of the damage accumulation mechanic, it wouldn't be Smash Bros.... I feel like it's more an issue of the game's 'play-feel,' rather than each minute aspect of the game."
- Key quotes:
  - "In the end my only hope is that the game reaches all kinds of people. I really want people who like eSports to be satisfied with the game, but I want beginners to feel the same way as well." — Masahiro Sakurai, Series Director, Famitsu/NintendoEverything interview, 2018
  - "To make things fair, you have to standardize many things. And when you do, every character ends up feeling the same." — Masahiro Sakurai, Series Director, DenFamiNicoGamer interview, 2018 (via EventHubs)
  - "Hitting the Fake Smash Ball and having a good laugh is what Smash Bros. is all about." — Masahiro Sakurai, Series Director, Famitsu/NintendoEverything interview, 2018
  - "Making 'everyone is here' a reality was honestly a miracle — and because of that, I think this is the only time we'll be able to do it." — Masahiro Sakurai, Series Director, Famitsu/NintendoEverything interview, 2018
- Why players love/support it: Every gaming memory is on the roster — your favorite character is in; chaos you can laugh through even when losing; a fighter anyone can pick up and nobody can fully master; and a director who visibly poured himself into honoring player love.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): No on-record statement found. ANALYSIS: Ultimate shipped complete at full price with two Fighters Passes as paid DLC; no microtransactions or loot boxes. The retention engine is roster love and infinite replayability, not purchases.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Never cut what players love for developer convenience — "Everyone Is Here" beats balanced; and protect the fun-over-fair instinct: silliness, items, and chaos that make losing fun are features, not flaws.
