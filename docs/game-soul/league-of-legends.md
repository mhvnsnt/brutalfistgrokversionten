# League of Legends — Heart and Soul

- Developer / studio: Riot Games
- Genre / platforms: Competitive MOBA (5v5 team-based) / PC, with console/mobile spin-off (Wild Rift)

- Why players come back (in the devs' view): The game is a "theme park" — a permanent evergreen core (Summoner's Rift) plus rotating experimental modes so both the ranked-core audience and the broader, more casual audience always find something they love. Player attachment to individual champions ("the champions themselves being the experience") is the main emotional hook. Retention beats acquisition: Riot grew by "not losing" players, not by buying them.

- Key quotes:
  - "Think of it like a theme park. Maybe you're just there for the Star Wars ride, and want to ride that over and over again... They are our core and we love our core. But we also want to start building out the Buzz Lightyear ride." — Pu Liu, Game Director, VG247, 2024
  - "We think those permanent fixtures serve evergreen motivations and cause people to keep coming back. They're very replayable, they are unique from the rest of the portfolio." — Pu Liu, Game Director, on evergreen vs rotating modes, VG247, 2024
  - "We grew not because we're great at acquiring players. We're great at not losing them." — Marc Merrill, Co-founder, on Riot's retention-first growth, Lemonade Stand podcast via Inven Global, 2026
  - "You're always having to learn to stay at the top of your game. You're always having to adapt to be the best player you can be." — Tom Cadwell, Designer, on the shifting metagame giving players reasons to return, PCGamesN [unverified]

- Why players love/support it: Champion affinity — players identify with "their" champion and express themselves through it across many modes; a permanently evolving metagame that rewards mastery and adaptation; regular novelty via rotating modes and events without breaking the core.

- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): "Sustainable, not infinitely profitable" — each Riot game must stay healthy and break even or better; the point is to keep serving players. "Marketing is irrelevant if you don't retain players" — Merrill, Lemonade Stand podcast via Inven Global, 2026. Free-to-play with cosmetic sales; no pay-to-win power.

- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Concrete Dragon needs its evergreen core (the beat-em-up loop) PLUS a rotating novelty layer (limited modes, events, districts) so long-timers get breaks without leaving; design characters players attach to by name, not just mechanics.
