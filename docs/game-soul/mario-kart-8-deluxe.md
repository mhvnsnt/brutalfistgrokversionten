# Mario Kart 8 Deluxe — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Nintendo EPD (producer Kosuke Yabuki, producer Hideki Konno)
- Genre / platforms: Kart racer (arcade/party racing); Wii U (2014 as Mario Kart 8), Switch (2017 as Deluxe)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Producer Kosuke Yabuki says the core of Mario Kart's appeal is that "you spend races throwing shells at them and trying to make them slip on banana peels — those unique tactics are the core of Mario Kart's appeal." It's "both an accessible and deep game": players who only play on Christmas sit beside players who grind every day, and the team balances for both. The design goal was emotions beyond the race result — "We wanted the game to be a fun experience for everyone. It can be frustrating to lose, but there's plenty that happens during races to make players crack a smile or want to play another round... We designed the game in such a way where unthinkable things like slipping on a banana just before the finish line happen one after the other." The lodestar is Iwata's "from 5 to 95 years old": steering assist and accessibility features pull new players in "one step at a time," while past developers' description of Mario Kart as "a competitive communication tool" is something the team says they'll always treasure.
- Key quotes:
  - "Generally speaking, you're not trying to hinder your opponent in racing games, but in Mario Kart, you spend races throwing shells at them and trying to make them slip on banana peels. Those unique tactics are the core of Mario Kart's appeal." — Kosuke Yabuki, Producer, Nikkei interview, 2022 (via NintendoEverything/Nintendo Life translation)
  - "We wanted the game to be a fun experience for everyone. It can be frustrating to lose, but there's plenty that happens during races to make players crack a smile or want to play another round." — Kosuke Yabuki, Producer, Nikkei interview, 2022
  - "Former Nintendo president Satoru Iwata often mentioned the phrase 'from 5 to 95 years old'. The ultimate goal is something that anyone can enjoy." — Kosuke Yabuki, Producer, Nikkei interview, 2022
  - "Players are going to have their own memories with each course, so we were careful not to change them too much... We want to honor players' memories while also polishing the allure of each course as IP." — Kosuke Yabuki, Producer, on the Booster Course Pass remasters, Nikkei interview, 2022
- Why players love/support it: Instant fun with anyone — split the Joy-Con and a sibling is racing in seconds; chaotic items that keep every race unpredictable until the final corner; deep enough for daily grinders, simple enough for holiday visitors; and a steady stream of new tracks (Booster Course Pass) that respects players' memories.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): No on-record statement found beyond the DLC framing. ANALYSIS: Mario Kart 8 Deluxe sold as a complete premium game; the Booster Course Pass added 48 remastered tracks as paid DLC framed as honoring players' memories — value through content, no consumables or pay-to-win items.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Design for emotions beyond the outcome — chaos and laughter keep people hitting "one more race" more than fair competition does; and chase "5 to 95" accessibility so a newcomer and a veteran can share the same session.
