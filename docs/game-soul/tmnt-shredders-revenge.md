# Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles: Shredder's Revenge — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Tribute Games (dev) + Dotemu (publisher) — co-founder/designer Jonathan Lavigne, designer Fred Gémus; published with Nickelodeon
- Genre / platforms: Arcade beat-'em-up / Switch, PS4/PS5, Xbox One/Series, PC, mobile
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Two convictions. First, **pacing is the genre's soul**: "what made the combat fun wasn't necessarily the combat moves themselves... it was more like the pacing of the game... getting that pacing and that rhythm right was really important" (Lavigne, Polygon, 2022). Second, **build it for everyone, not just the hardcore**: Tribute initially wanted "retro hard"; Dotemu pushed for a game for everyone and "the result was infinitely more enjoyable" — "Minimizing frustrations and putting emphasis on the couch co-op and the multiplayer experience really took the game to another level." The depth is there but hidden inside simple inputs ("It's all simple stuff to pull off, but there's a lot of depth" — juggles, ground bounces, cancels), and enemy design forces technique: blockers force charged attacks/grabs ("push what a typical button-mashing beat 'em up is"). And they design "how you remember [the classics] rather than exactly how they are" — nostalgia is the *memory*, improved: modern quality-of-life like auto-aligning hits to the player's *intent* ("try to figure out what you intended to do and slightly align your character for you, so the hit connects"). Replayability via story mode, challenges, arcade mode, and a scaling co-op roster that grows to 7 characters.
- Key quotes:
  - "We noticed from the classic [TMNT] games that what made the combat fun wasn't necessarily the combat moves themselves, or the combat mechanics, it was more like the pacing of the game. The game[s] played really fast, and the way enemies would enter the screen [in formation] and be defeated was really quick. So getting that pacing and that rhythm right was really important." — Jonathan Lavigne, Co-founder/Designer, Polygon interview (2022)
  - "We love to make games how you remember them rather than exactly how they are because sometimes they can be rough and limited." — Fred Gémus, Designer, CBR interview (2022)
  - "The toughest part in reviving old memories is striking the perfect balance between old and new... Today, we need to implement systems that will try to figure out what you intended to do and slightly align your character for you, so the hit connects." — Jean-François Major, Co-founder, ComicBook interview (2022)
  - "Minimizing frustrations and putting emphasis on the couch co-op and the multiplayer experience really took the game to another level." — Tribute Games dev (via Inverse interview, 2022) [unverified — speaker name not fully confirmed from snippet]
- Why players love/support it: It's a playable episode of the 1987 cartoon (each stage "as if it was its own distinct episode"), with the original opening theme and returning '87 voice actors; fast "rush to play" combat pacing; up to 6-player co-op with adaptive difficulty ("the game also adapts itself according to the number of players in real-time"); each turtle plays distinct (range/speed/power stats); it earned a 1M+ seller and DLC (Dimension Shellshock).
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Premium game + expansion DLC (Dimension Shellshock — new mode + characters) sold on quality. No on-record developer statements found about a monetization philosophy beyond premium-package pricing; the model is premium + substantial expansions.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Make games "how players remember the classics, not exactly how they were" — nostalgia is the memory, and the job is to modernize under it. And optimize for couch co-op first: minimizing frustration for casuals doesn't lose the hardcore, it grows the whole room.
