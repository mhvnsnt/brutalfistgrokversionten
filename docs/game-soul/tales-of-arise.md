# Tales of Arise — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Bandai Namco Studios (Tales of Studio lineage); producer Yusuke Tomizawa
- Genre / platforms: Action JRPG; PS5/PS4/Xbox Series/Xbox One/PC (2021) + Beyond the Dawn expansion (2023)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Character drama, singular and personal — "So this game is really a standalone game where one person really enjoys the drama and the overall story of the game" (Tomizawa, IGN). The design philosophy: "all entries in the Tales series mirror the relationships between the characters within the gameplay" — Alphen's painlessness + Shionne's thorns was "chosen because, although it was a challenge from a game design standpoint, it was a very appealing setting from a dramatic standpoint." Players stayed for the romance so strongly that Tomizawa got "many requests from people who really enjoyed the game and wanted to know more about... their future," which directly led to the Beyond the Dawn expansion.
- Key quotes: (2-4; each: "quote" — Name, Role, Publication/Event, Year; append [unverified] if from a search snippet not a fetched page)
  - "The relationship between Alphen and Shionne was mainly a drama-based idea. Various ideas were proposed to make the relationship between the two unique, but the relationship between painlessness and thorns was chosen because, although it was a challenge from a game design standpoint, it was a very appealing setting from a dramatic standpoint." — Yusuke Tomizawa, Producer, Inverse, 2022
  - "For me personally, the only thing that matters in both cases is that the irreplaceable choices made by the characters as they face the challenges of their world create emotion and a good experience for the player." — Yusuke Tomizawa, Producer, Inverse, 2022
  - "So this game is really a standalone game where one person really enjoys the drama and the overall story of the game, we don't really have any plans currently for a multiplayer mode. But on the other side, we did put a lot of focus on how the characters work together and how they cooperate in the battle and how that plays out in game mechanics." — Yusuke Tomizawa, Producer, IGN (via Push Square), 2021 [unverified]
- Why players love/support it: It is Bandai Namco's "Romeo and Juliet" — a star-crossed romance where the drama is literally playable (Alphen burns his own HP with the Blazing Sword; Shionne heals it back). It was the first Tales in a long while with "a clear-cut happy ending," which fans celebrated. Requests for more of Alphen and Shionne's future became the expansion.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Single-purchase premium; paid expansion (Beyond the Dawn) built from direct fan demand, not a roadmap obligation — "we received many requests from people who really enjoyed the game and wanted to know more." No sequel milking of the couple's arc: "we did not create the game with a sequel in mind."
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Mirror character relationships inside the gameplay systems (their drama changes how they fight) — and when players ask for more of a beloved story, build the expansion for them; demand-first DLC beats roadmap DLC.
