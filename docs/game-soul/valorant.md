# Valorant — Heart and Soul

- Developer / studio: Riot Games
- Genre / platforms: Tactical 5v5 hero shooter / PC, PS5, Xbox Series X|S

- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Riot expects and embraces "seasonality" — players naturally take breaks and return; the job is to keep shipping variety (agents, maps, events) so there's always a reason to come back, rather than panicking over dips. The game is positioned as a serious, mastery-driven tactical shooter that "scratches a different itch" than hero-action shooters, so its identity never blurs. No end in sight: "I don't really see an end to the journey for us."

- Key quotes:
  - "Games - both genres and specific games - have this ebb and flow. I think it's healthy. Like any other game, we see players take a break from [Valorant], then come back, and so you have these natural 'seasonalities' in how many players are playing and engaging." — Arnar Gylfason, Production Director, PCGamesN, 2024
  - "I don't really see an end to [the Valorant] journey for us." — Arnar Gylfason, Production Director, PCGamesN, 2024
  - "At the core, we are a tactical shooter. We happen to have agents who have abilities, which gives us a lot of liberty and leeway to create all kinds of gameplay fantasies." — Arnar Gylfason, Production Director, GamesRadar+ via TheGamer, 2024 [unverified]
  - "That doesn't mean infinitely profitable, [it] means sustainable because the whole point is keep serving players." — Marc Merrill, Co-founder, on Riot's evergreen strategy covering Valorant, Lemonade Stand podcast via Inven Global, 2026

- Why players love/support it: Precision-first gunplay with a skill ceiling that rewards years of mastery; clutch moments that become shared stories; agent personalities (voice lines, relationships) that make the world feel alive beyond the match; a thriving esports circuit (VCT) giving players a dream to chase.

- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Cosmetic-only (skins, gun buddies, player cards) — no agent/ability purchase affects gameplay; agents earnable free. Merrill's evergreen doctrine: sustainable, break-even-or-better, keep serving players — no direct on-record Valorant-specific monetization quote found.

- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Plan for seasonality — design comeback hooks (events, new districts, unlocks) instead of fearing player dips. Define the game's identity sharply (Concrete Dragon = beat-em-up first) and don't chase trends that blur it.
