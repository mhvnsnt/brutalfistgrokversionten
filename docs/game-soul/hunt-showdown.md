# Hunt: Showdown — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Crytek
- Genre / platforms: PvPvE extraction horror FPS; PC, PS4/PS5, Xbox (premium buy-to-play; 1896 relaunch 2024)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): The tension loop — long stretches of intense, concentration-draining creeping through the Bayou, then explosive release when careful plans collapse into messy, cathartic, heart-in-mouth gunfights; a fantasy period-piece arsenal (Wild West meets early industrial age) makes every shot weighty and memorable; "the real monsters are other humans" — unpredictable human hunters generate moments no AI could; a community-driven roadmap where player feedback is "the essential driving part" and features that don't fit the match-to-match experience get killed (e.g. a scrapped crafting system); live events as testing grounds for new mechanics.
- Key quotes:
  - "I'm not surprised, but I mean I was hoping for it, honestly. Being able to continue working on it is a huge privilege for us." — Dennis Schwarz, Lead Designer, Crytek, NME (2023)
  - "We have weapons which are one foot in the Wild West and one foot in the modern industrial age. This time period has seen so much transitioning in terms of what firearms mean and how you conduct warfare." — Dennis Schwarz, Lead Designer, Crytek, NME (2023)
  - "The individual shot is a lot more important than, say, another magazine." — Dennis Schwarz, Lead Designer, Crytek, NME (2023)
  - "We saw how people play *Hunt: Showdown* and how the game found its formula. It felt like it would get in the way of the match-to-match experience that we had." — Dennis Schwarz, Lead Designer, Crytek, NME (2023; on scrapping the planned crafting system)
- Why players love/support it: Nothing else delivers Hunt's tension-release cycle — players call it the most nerve-shredding FPS ever; permadeath hunters make every engagement genuinely costly; the sound design and grimy 1895 atmosphere are peerless; a hardcore community that stays through controversies because the core experience is irreplaceable.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): No on-record developer statement on monetization philosophy found in this pass. ANALYSIS: premium buy-to-play, no free-to-play conversion (pre-launch "won't be free-to-play" statement on record); cosmetic DLC and event passes fund continued development.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Engineer the tension loop deliberately — long slow burn of anticipation punctured by cathartic release is more addictive than constant action. And let the community kill features: if a system (even one you planned) fights the core loop, scrap it — players trust a studio that deprioritizes its own roadmap for their experience.
