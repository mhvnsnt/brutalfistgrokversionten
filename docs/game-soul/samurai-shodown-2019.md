# Samurai Shodown (2019) — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: SNK (producer Yasuyuki Oda; game/art director Nobuyuki Kuroki; game designer Joshua Weatherford)
- Genre / platforms: Weapons-based 3D fighting game (2D plane) / PS4, Xbox One, Switch, PC (Steam/Epic), Stadia
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): SamSho 2019 is a study in *deliberate anti-modernism*: slow, high-risk, high-damage, spacing-and-mind-reading combat where "death is just one strike away." The devs deliberately refused to modernize: "It's not really modernized, it is really faithful to the old ones because we feel like it stands out... the high-damage, high-risk, high-reward nature of the game" (DualShockers, PAX East 2019). Oda banked on the game feeling so *different* that it would attract players who never belonged to the fighting-game scene: "people come out of nowhere that haven't been on the scene before, who resonate with this game that's so different from other games, and make a new entrance." Character uniqueness is the design law — "there's not really a single clone character... all of them really stand on their own" (Oda, Gematsu, 2019) — and movesets were preserved wholesale for returning players ("almost none of that [move-cutting] happened here"). Community gratitude is the operating model: "SNK deeply values and appreciates its community... by the revival of KOF, SAMURAI SHODOWN, and other old titles, we can show our gratitude to the community and our fans" (Kuroki, Siliconera, 2020).
- Key quotes:
  - "It's not really modernized, it is really faithful to the old ones because we feel like it stands out, that the general systems stand out, because of the high-damage, high-risk, high-reward nature of the game." — SNK dev team, DualShockers interview, PAX East 2019
  - "The system in general is just so simple and easy to pick up that people will jump right into it. And you might see a lot of people come out of nowhere that haven't been on the scene before, who resonate with this game that's so different from other games, and make a new entrance." — Yasuyuki Oda, Producer, Gematsu interview (Mar 2019)
  - "Also, there's not really a single clone character—that's always been one of the things about Samurai Shodown. Even Hanzo and Galford, two ninjas that should play alike, play nothing alike... all of them really stand on their own." — Yasuyuki Oda, Producer, Gematsu interview (Mar 2019)
  - "In any case, we depend on the community for bringing hype to our games, and so we wish to do right by them." — Nobuyuki Kuroki, Director, Siliconera interview (2020)
- Why players love/support it: The fear of one big sword swing — "the terror of fighting weapon-to-weapon" (series philosophy, Polygon 2017 interview with the original team's Ya); every round a tense, Kurosawa-like duel; Dojo Mode's AI ghosts of real players; the faithful roster of beloved 1993-cast veterans; season passes priced fairly (Season 1 free for early buyers).
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Season pass of 4 returning characters was FREE for buyers in the first week of launch — "As a thank you to the fans who have stuck with us during the long wait" (SNK, via Bleeding Cool, 2019). Retention-through-gratitude: thank the faithful early adopters, then sell subsequent seasons.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Being *different on purpose* is a growth strategy — a slow, heavy, high-stakes brawler can attract players who bounce off combo-racing fighters. And make every character mechanically unique; "no clone characters" is a retention feature, not a luxury.
