# Final Fantasy XIV — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Square Enix (Creative Business Unit III)
- Genre / platforms: MMORPG / PC, PlayStation, Xbox
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Director/producer Naoki Yoshida's formula is explicit: "what makes a hit MMO is that, do you want to return to that game or not; that's the most important thing. To do that you need to create a lot of content... a strong community... frequent updates with value. But when it comes down to it, it's going to be, 'is the world fun that people are going to return and to spend a lot of time.'" Underneath it all is trust: after the disastrous 2010 launch, Yoshida rebuilt FFXIV around one goal — "regain the trust of the players and the fans who were disappointed with the game" — and "recovering the trust from the players is the most important thing — then the business will follow." Story-first design matters too: before it's an MMORPG it's an RPG and a Final Fantasy game, so new players can "play our story, to play it like you would a no-MMO Final Fantasy game." His success rule: "The most important attribute is not giving up; never give up."
- Key quotes:
  - "For me what makes a hit MMO is that, do you want to return to that game or not; that's the most important thing. To do that you need to create a lot of content, you need to have things that players can do in your game. You also need to create a strong community. You also need frequent updates with value." — Naoki Yoshida, Director/Producer (Gamereactor, Gamescom 2018)
  - "When FFXIV originally launched in 2010 and it ended up being a failure, rather than setting a 'numbers' goal, our main focus was to regain the trust of the players and the fans who were disappointed with the game. We were going at it full force, to rebuild it." — Naoki Yoshida, Director/Producer (PCGamesN, 2015)
  - "The most important attribute is not giving up; never give up." — Naoki Yoshida, Director/Producer (PCGamesN, 2015)
  - "We would like the players to form their opinions by playing the game... I want to look at more business opportunities... But, to do that, recovering the trust from the players is the most important thing — then the business will follow." — Naoki Yoshida, Director/Producer (RPG Site interview, 2011)
- Why players love/support it: Unparalleled story-driven MMO content, a reputation for developer honesty (regular Letter from the Producer livestreams), and a subscription model that keeps the game the product rather than the player's wallet.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Yoshida stood as "a defiant subscription holdout" against the free-to-play wave, arguing free-to-play pivots often mean the makers "decided to give up"; he suspended billing entirely from 2010 to 2012 while the team proved itself, only resuming subscriptions when they felt they could decently charge (PCGamesN, 2015).
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Earn trust before chasing numbers — never charge for something players don't yet believe in; make the world/story fun enough that "do I want to return" answers itself, then let revenue follow the trust; never give up on a game players still love.
