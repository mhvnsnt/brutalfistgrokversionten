# Borderlands 3 — Heart and Soul
- Developer / studio: Gearbox Software (2K Games)
- Genre / platforms: Looter-shooter FPS-RPG; PC, PS4/PS5, Xbox (2019)
- Why players come back (in the devs' view): Gearbox has treated its games as a "hobby" since day one — things players "engage with and interact with over time," not consume once and drop; dedicated live-game team invested before launch, free events and online content alongside paid campaign DLC; the studio deliberately buffs weak builds instead of nerfing strong ones so players always "feel like gods"; the loot chase plus co-op sandbox keeps the grind social and joyful.
- Key quotes:
  - "We wanted people to interact with our games, not as something that you quickly play, consume, and put down, but that they can engage with and interact with over time." — Randy Pitchford, Founder/CEO and Executive Producer, Gearbox Software, GameSpot, 2020
  - "You can buy it, you can be confident that there's value there, and you don't feel like you're going to be nickel and dimed on microtransactions or get latched into a subscription model that hits you up for loot boxes." — Randy Pitchford, Founder/CEO and Executive Producer, Gearbox Software, GameSpot, 2020
  - "Our goal is to create a lot of joy, and happiness, and positive experiences, and that you come out of it, as an audience member, thinking you got the better end of the deal." — Randy Pitchford, Founder/CEO and Executive Producer, Gearbox Software, GameSpot, 2020
  - "We want players to become gods and feel like gods. It's not cool to just knock them down. Instead of weakening strong builds, we make the weaker ones stronger." [unverified] — Randy Pitchford, CEO, Gearbox Software, Gameranx (Tokyo Game Show, 2025; search snippet only)
- Why players love/support it: The "bazillions of guns" fantasy delivers — every session can drop something absurd and exciting; co-op loot-sharing is a friend-group ritual; Gearbox's anti-predatory stance earned real goodwill; long-tail support kept Borderlands 2 at 50–100k daily players eight years after launch (per Pitchford), and the franchise rewards that loyalty with meaty campaign DLC.
- Monetization philosophy (devs' words, if any): Pitchford stated it directly (above): no microtransaction nickel-and-diming, no subscriptions, no loot boxes; sell the game with "incredible value," then offer campaign DLC/season passes players can buy with confidence. He framed it as an entertainer's contract — the audience should feel they got the better end of the deal — and called predatory "Skinner box" design something the industry shouldn't do.
- Lesson for our games (1-2 lines, concrete): Make every player feel they got the better end of the deal — generous value first, paid content as honest additions, never as Skinner boxes. And when a build is too strong, buff everything else instead of nerfing it: players stay for the power fantasy, not the patch notes.
